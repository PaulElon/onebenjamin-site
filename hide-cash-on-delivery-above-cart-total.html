How to hide Cash on Delivery above a cart total in Shopify

Cash on Delivery (COD) is fine for small orders. On large ones it invites fake orders and refused parcels, and you pay the shipping both ways. Merchants ask for the same fix again and again:

"How can I limit the maximum order price on cash on delivery? I am getting fake orders of billions of rupees." (Shopify Community)

This guide covers what Shopify does on its own, the workarounds, and how an app does it at checkout.

Short answer

Shopify's admin has no setting to hide a payment method when the cart is above an amount. Under Settings > Payments, a manual method like COD is either on for every order or off for every order. To hide it by cart total you need a payment customization: a small rule that runs inside Shopify checkout (a Shopify Function) and removes the method from the list when your condition is true.

Which Shopify plans this works on

Old forum answers say you need Shopify Plus. For a public app, Shopify's help center says otherwise:

  • Basic or higher. "Stores on the Basic plan or higher, but not on the Shopify Plus plan, still have access to payment method customization apps." Public apps built with Shopify Functions work on Basic and Plus; custom apps built with Functions are Plus only.
  • United States and Canada, non-Plus: "these customizations are limited to non-credit card payment methods." Cash on Delivery is a manual method, not a credit card, so a COD rule is not blocked by this limit.

Source: Shopify Help Center, Customizing payment methods and delivery options at checkout, checked 2026-11-22.

How a payment customization rule works

  1. The customer reaches checkout. Shopify runs the function with the cart: subtotal, currency, shipping country, and customer.
  2. The rule checks the condition, for example "subtotal is above 200".
  3. If it is true, the function tells Shopify to hide the method named "Cash on Delivery (COD)". The customer sees the other methods only.

Because it runs in checkout itself, a customer cannot get around it by editing the cart page, using a direct checkout link, or reloading. Nothing is added to your theme, so a theme update cannot break it.

Things that trip people up

  • The name must match. The rule finds the method by the name customers see at checkout. "Cash on Delivery (COD)" is a name Shopify reserves for its own suggested COD method, so if you made a custom method instead (for example "Pay on delivery"), the rule must use that name.
  • Test with a second method on. In our own test, hiding a store's only payment method had no effect. Enable a card or bank method before you test.
  • Subtotal or total? Decide whether shipping and discounts count. Most rules use the subtotal.
  • Currency. One merchant noticed that "by selecting COD the prices will automatically be reverted from the local currency to the store’s main currency." Shopify says manual methods display in each market's currency only with Shopify Payments and its international sales tools turned on. If you sell in several currencies, test your threshold in each one.
  • Checkout only, not product pages. These rules act on the payment step of checkout. They do not remove buy buttons on product or cart pages.
  • Wallets. Shopify lets functions remove wallets such as Apple Pay but not reorder or rename them.

Make COD the default, or move it down

Some merchants want the opposite: COD shown first because most of their buyers pay that way. Others want it kept but listed last. Shopify's admin cannot change the order of payment methods, but a payment customization can reorder them as well as hide or rename them. You can combine rules, for example "COD first below 200, hidden above 200".

Can I charge a fee for COD instead?

Not natively. Shopify has no setting that adds a surcharge when a buyer picks Cash on Delivery, and a higher plan does not unlock one. A payment customization cannot do it either: it can rename, reorder, or hide payment methods, set payment terms, and add a review requirement, but it cannot change the order total. That includes our app.

The workarounds merchants use, and their catch:

  • A shipping rate labeled for COD, priced with the fee built in. The fee shows before payment, but nothing stops a buyer from picking that rate and then paying by card.
  • A fee product or fee app that adds a line item. Check what happens when a buyer switches from COD to card: some setups leave the fee in place, or add it only after the order is placed.
  • No fee, just a ceiling or a floor. If the fee is meant to cover refused parcels, hiding COD above a cart total (or below a small one) removes the riskiest orders without touching the total. This is the rule this guide is about.

Do not rename COD to mention a fee that is not actually charged. Buyers read the label as a promise. The same goes for partial COD (a deposit now, the rest at the door): hiding a method does not split a payment.

Sources: Shopify's developer docs, Payment Customization Function API (operations: rename, reorder, hide, payment terms, review requirement), checked 2026-11-15; Shopify Community: "Shopify Native COD (Cash on Delivery) fee", September 2026.

How to test a COD rule before Black Friday

  1. Keep a second payment method on, then add items below your limit and go to checkout. COD should show.
  2. Add items until the subtotal is above the limit. COD should disappear.
  3. Apply a discount code that brings the cart back under the limit, and check the result matches the rule you wrote down.
  4. Repeat for each country and currency you sell in.

Currency and reserved-name facts: Shopify Help Center, Manual payment methods, checked 2026-11-22. Quote: Shopify Community, "Use local currency for COD", March 2025.

Limits worth knowing before you install anything

  • 25 rules per store. Shopify activates at most 25 payment customization functions on one store, across all apps. If you already run several payment apps, count them.
  • Online checkout only. The rules run at checkout and in checkouts opened from a draft order invoice. They do not run in Shopify POS or on draft orders completed in the admin.
  • Wallets. Express buttons such as Apple Pay and Google Pay can be hidden, but are not reordered or renamed like ordinary methods.

Source: Shopify's developer docs, Payment Customization Function API ("You can activate a maximum of 25 payment customization functions on each store"; supported surfaces list), checked 2026-11-14.

Our app

OneBen Payment Rules hides, renames, or moves payment methods at checkout by cart subtotal, shipping country, or customer tag. It runs as a Shopify Function with no theme code. A hide rule for "Cash on Delivery (COD)" above a subtotal is the main use.

What Payment Rules can't hide or change

  • Draft orders completed in the admin, order edits, and POS. Shopify does not run payment customization functions there. Shopify lists draft order invoice checkouts as supported, but some merchants report payment-hiding apps not applying to invoices, and we have not tested it ourselves. Test one invoice before you rely on it.
  • Methods inside Shop Pay. Once a buyer is in Shop Pay, Shopify applies no hide, rename, or move to its payment methods.
  • Express buttons. A hide rule removes a wallet such as Apple Pay or Google Pay from every place it shows in checkout (express buttons and the payment list); the app does not hide one placement only. Wallets cannot be renamed or moved. We have not yet tested wallet rules on a real checkout.
  • Methods Shopify restricts for your plan or country. Shopify ignores rules that target a restricted method. One competitor says credit cards on non-Plus US and Canada stores are restricted; we have not verified that, so test the exact method on your store.
  • Billing country and storefront country. Country rules read the shipping address only.
